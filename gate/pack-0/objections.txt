Every week, the small town held a busy market in the old square by the church. Farmers came early in the morning with their carts of apples, cabbages and onions, and a baker set out fresh loaves and sweet buns on a long table. At the next stall, the carrots were piled high in wooden boxes, bright [[blue|orange]] with their leafy tops still on. They sold quickly to the cooks from the big houses.

A woman sold fresh eggs and butter from a basket lined with straw. A man with a small cart sold hot chestnuts in paper cones, at a penny a cone. Beside him stood heavy sacks of new potatoes, fresh from the [[treetops|ground]] of his farm, and a scale for weighing them.

By late morning the square was crowded and noisy. Then, little by little, the crowd thinned, and the farmers loaded their empty crates back onto the carts and set off along the country roads toward home.
