A canal lock lets boats climb or descend a slope of land. It is a long stone chamber with a pair of heavy wooden gates at each end.

To go up, a boat enters the lower end, and the keeper closes the gates behind it. Water from the higher reach then flows in through small openings in the upper gates, and the boat rises slowly with the level. When the water inside matches the water above, the upper gates swing open, and the boat moves on. Going down works the same way in reverse, as the water drains out through the lower gates. A busy lock might pass dozens of boats in a single day, and on some canals, long flights of locks stand one after another up the side of a hill.
